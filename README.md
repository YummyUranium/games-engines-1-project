### Project name: Place In Space

## Project Description
Imagine you're standing in the middle of some large public space, like Grangegorman or Phoenix Park or O'Connell Street. You close your eyes and point to some landmark, like the Spire. You open your eyes. How close are you?

This is one of the strengths of virtual reality that I want to explore - testing how good the user is at spatial awareness and perception. I like to think that my sense of direction is pretty good. Why don't I make a video game to find out just how good it is? This project is partially inspired by a website called ![Human Benchmark](www.humanbenchmark.com), which has a large number of brain games and cognitive tests. I want to extend the same principle into 3D space. What I love about Human Benchmark is its simplicity: the addictiveness comes from your own brain engaging with the tasks and wanting to improve your score, and it shows what percentile of the user population your score places you in, giving you a competitive incentive to do better.

The three minigames are as follows:
### Compass
You park your car in a crowded petrol station and go to get a chicken fillet roll. With nothing but some landmarks around the carpark, you must place your car as accurately as possible. (chicken fillet roll sold seperately)
Harder levels include - being a librarian and having to point to a specific book on a shelf, having to point from your current location on earth to big cities like New York or Shanghai, and accurately predicting the trajectory of a ball as it is mid-throw.
### Ruler
How long is a foot, anyway? In this gamemode, you have to eyeball how far various distances are, from inches to meters to yards in front of you, and kilometres and knots on a map.
### Map
The floor is lava, but there is a safe path across! Unfortunately, I forgot to give the collision mesh a colour, so it's invisible. Luckily, you've memorised the path before you set out. The harder the tests get, the bigger the grid.
